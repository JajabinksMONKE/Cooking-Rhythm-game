# Lunar New Year Pixel Pack - Art Guide

For artists and level designers: what each item is called in Chinese, what it means in Chinese New Year culture, and where to place it in a game scene. Many New Year items carry a lucky meaning, often from a pun on their Chinese name, so placing them in the right spot makes a scene feel authentic. For import settings, see **Technical_Guide.md**.

## Scene recipes

Combinations that work well together. **Example_Festive_Front_Door.png** shows the first recipe: the pack's decorations turning an ordinary game house door into a festive Chinese New Year entrance, and **Example_Reunion_Dinner.png** shows the reunion dinner (the house gate, table, walls and floors are only a backdrop and are not part of the pack).

- **Festive front door:** Temple_Door (or your own house door) + Fu_Sign on the door + Door_God_Poster ×2 + Couplet_Strip ×2 on the sides + Couplet_Header above + Lantern_Red or Lantern_Palace ×2 under the eaves + Stone_Lion ×2 (mirrored) in front.
- **New Year market street:** Lantern_Arch at the entrance + Market_Stall in rows + Lantern_String across the street + Lantern_Standing along the sides + Tanghulu, Baozi_Steamer and Fireworks_Crate on the stalls.
- **Reunion dinner:** A table with Dumplings_Plate, Steamed_Fish, Hot_Pot, Spring_Rolls and Orange_Plate; Candy_Box and Tea_Set nearby; Window_Papercut and Chinese_Knot on the walls; Mandarin_Tree in the corner.
- **Temple courtyard:** Paifang_Gate as the entrance + Incense_Burner in the centre + Offering_Table at the hall + Big_Drum and Gong_Stand + Wishing_Plaque_Rack and Plum_Tree on the sides + Fortune_Sticks as an interaction.
- **Festival night:** Firework_Red and Firework_Gold in the sky + Sky_Lantern floating + Dragon_Dance_Head or Lion_Dance_Head on the square + Firecracker_Chain and Firecracker_Pop at the doors.

## Items

### Decorations

Hung on walls, doors, eaves and streets.

#### Round red lantern — 红灯笼 (hóng dēnglong)

`images/Decorations/Lantern_Red.png` · 128x128 px

- **Meaning:** Red stands for luck and keeps evil away; lanterns bring light, reunion and a prosperous year.
- **Where to place it:** Hang from eaves, gate beams or lantern posts. Use it as a large centrepiece above a main entrance or in the middle of a festival square.

#### Palace lantern — 宫灯 (gōngdēng)

`images/Decorations/Lantern_Palace.png` · 64x128 px

- **Meaning:** Originally made for the imperial palace, so it stands for nobility, wealth and a flourishing household.
- **Where to place it:** Hang under the eaves of palaces, temples and rich houses, usually as a pair on both sides of a door.

#### Lantern string — 灯笼串 (dēnglong chuàn)

`images/Decorations/Lantern_String.png` · 192x64 px

- **Meaning:** Rows of lanterns mean a lively, bustling festival and a bright year ahead.
- **Where to place it:** Stretch between two posts or buildings across a street. Place several side by side to make a long garland.

#### Chinese knot — 中国结 (Zhōngguó jié)

`images/Decorations/Chinese_Knot.png` · 64x128 px

- **Meaning:** Tied from one single cord with no beginning or end: unity, harmony and endless good luck.
- **Where to place it:** Hang on walls, doors, pillars or beside windows. Good on any house that celebrates the festival.

#### Fu door sign — 福字 / 倒福 (fú zì / dào fú)

`images/Decorations/Fu_Sign.png` · 64x64 px

- **Meaning:** 福 means good fortune and blessings. It is hung upside down because 倒 (upside down) sounds like 到 (arrived): "good fortune has arrived".
- **Where to place it:** Put it on the front door, centred. Traditionally it is often hung upside down (flip it vertically): an upside-down 福 means "good fortune has arrived".

#### Spring couplet strip — 春联 (chūnlián)

`images/Decorations/Couplet_Strip.png` · 64x128 px

- **Meaning:** A pair of poetic lines with wishes for the new year, pasted on both sides of the door. Red paper scares away Nian, the New Year monster. Couplets always come in pairs; this image is one of the two strips.
- **Where to place it:** Use two, one on each side of a doorway (mirror one horizontally if you like). Combine with Couplet_Header above the door.

#### Couplet header — 横批 (héngpī)

`images/Decorations/Couplet_Header.png` · 128x64 px

- **Meaning:** The horizontal scroll above the door that sums up the wish of the couplet.
- **Where to place it:** Place horizontally above a doorway, between the two Couplet_Strip pieces.

#### Window paper-cut — 窗花 (chuānghuā)

`images/Decorations/Window_Papercut.png` · 64x64 px

- **Meaning:** Paper-cut window flowers; symmetrical blossoms wish for a beautiful, abundant new year.
- **Where to place it:** Stick on house windows or plain walls; one per window.

#### Firecracker chain — 鞭炮 (biānpào)

`images/Decorations/Firecracker_Chain.png` · 64x128 px

- **Meaning:** The loud bangs chase away the monster Nian and bad luck, and welcome the new year.
- **Where to place it:** Hang from eaves, door frames or poles, especially near gates and shop entrances at the start of the festival.

#### Embroidered silk ball — 绣球 (xiùqiú)

`images/Decorations/Silk_Ball.png` · 64x64 px

- **Meaning:** An embroidered ball used in celebrations (and traditionally thrown to choose a husband): happiness, love and luck.
- **Where to place it:** Hang from eaves, pavilions or stage beams as a celebration ornament. Also works as a quest or reward item.

#### Door god poster — 门神 (ménshén)

`images/Decorations/Door_God_Poster.png` · 64x96 px

- **Meaning:** Guardian generals pasted on the front doors to protect the home from evil spirits.
- **Where to place it:** Use a pair on the two leaves of a double front door, mirrored so the guardians face each other.

### Props

Ground objects for streets, temples and squares.

#### Standing lantern post — 灯笼柱 (dēnglong zhù)

`images/Props/Lantern_Standing.png` · 64x128 px

- **Meaning:** A lantern lighting the way: guidance and a bright future.
- **Where to place it:** Line streets, paths and temple approaches, roughly every 2-3 tiles.

#### Big festival drum — 大鼓 (dàgǔ)

`images/Props/Big_Drum.png` · 128x128 px

- **Meaning:** Drumming drives away evil and announces celebrations; its beat leads the lion and dragon dances.
- **Where to place it:** Town square, festival stage or temple courtyard; the heart of a lion dance or parade area.

#### Gong on stand — 锣 (luó)

`images/Props/Gong_Stand.png` · 128x128 px

- **Meaning:** A gong strike marks auspicious moments and scares off bad luck; gongs and drums together (锣鼓喧天) mean a lively celebration.
- **Where to place it:** Temple courtyard or performance stage, next to the Big_Drum.

#### Stone guardian lion — 石狮子 (shí shīzi)

`images/Props/Stone_Lion.png` · 64x128 px

- **Meaning:** Guardian lions protect buildings from evil. They come in pairs: the one with a ball stands for power, the one with a cub for family.
- **Where to place it:** Guard both sides of a gate, temple door or important building, as a mirrored pair.

#### Incense burner — 香炉 (xiānglú)

`images/Props/Incense_Burner.png` · 64x64 px

- **Meaning:** Burning incense carries prayers and respect to the gods and ancestors.
- **Where to place it:** In front of temples and shrines, or in the centre of a temple courtyard.

#### Lion dance head — 舞狮头 (wǔshī tóu)

`images/Props/Lion_Dance_Head.png` · 64x64 px

- **Meaning:** The lion dance brings good luck and scares away evil spirits; performed at New Year and shop openings.
- **Where to place it:** Festival stage, parade route or market display. Can also be a wearable, collectible or quest item.

#### Fireworks crate — 烟花箱 (yānhuā xiāng)

`images/Props/Fireworks_Crate.png` · 64x64 px

- **Meaning:** Fireworks celebrate and chase away bad luck; a full crate promises a big celebration.
- **Where to place it:** Market stalls, storage corners and open areas where a fireworks event takes place.

#### Offering table — 供桌 (gòngzhuō)

`images/Props/Offering_Table.png` · 128x64 px

- **Meaning:** Offerings to ancestors and gods show respect and ask for blessings in the new year.
- **Where to place it:** Inside or in front of temples, ancestral halls and home altars.

#### Lantern riddle board — 灯谜 (dēngmí)

`images/Props/Riddle_Board.png` · 128x128 px

- **Meaning:** Guessing riddles written on lanterns is a Lantern Festival (元宵节) tradition: a clever, playful game.
- **Where to place it:** Lantern festival or market area. Ideal for an interactive lantern-riddle minigame.

#### Wishing plaque rack — 许愿牌 (xǔyuàn pái)

`images/Props/Wishing_Plaque_Rack.png` · 128x128 px

- **Meaning:** People write wishes on red plaques and hang them up for the gods to grant.
- **Where to place it:** Temple grounds or beside a big tree. Ideal for an interactive "make a wish" spot.

### Food

Table dishes and festive treats (props or pickups).

#### Plate of dumplings — 饺子 (jiǎozi)

`images/Food/Dumplings_Plate.png` · 128x128 px

- **Meaning:** Shaped like ancient gold ingots, so they mean wealth; eaten at midnight on New Year's Eve for family reunion.
- **Where to place it:** Centrepiece of the New Year's Eve reunion dinner table; also kitchens and food stalls.

#### Bowl of tangyuan — 汤圆 (tāngyuán)

`images/Food/Tangyuan_Bowl.png` · 64x64 px

- **Meaning:** Round rice balls stand for family reunion and completeness (团圆); eaten at the Lantern Festival.
- **Where to place it:** Dining tables and dessert shops; the classic Lantern Festival dessert.

#### Baozi in steamer — 包子 (bāozi)

`images/Food/Baozi_Steamer.png` · 64x64 px

- **Meaning:** Rising dough means rising fortune (蒸蒸日上); a full steamer means abundance.
- **Where to place it:** Street food stalls, breakfast shops and kitchen counters.

#### Steamed fish — 鱼 (yú)

`images/Food/Steamed_Fish.png` · 128x64 px

- **Meaning:** 鱼 (fish) sounds like 余 (surplus): 年年有余, "may you have more than enough every year". Traditionally some is left uneaten.
- **Where to place it:** Centrepiece of the reunion dinner table (fish = "surplus every year").

#### Nian gao rice cake — 年糕 (niángāo)

`images/Food/Nian_Gao.png` · 64x64 px

- **Meaning:** Sounds like 年高, "higher every year": progress, promotion and growth.
- **Where to place it:** Dining tables, offering tables, or as a gift item when visiting neighbours.

#### Tanghulu — 糖葫芦 (tánghúlu)

`images/Food/Tanghulu.png` · 64x64 px

- **Meaning:** Sweet red candied fruit for a sweet life; 葫芦 also sounds like 福禄 (fortune and prosperity).
- **Where to place it:** Street vendors and markets; a handheld snack pickup.

#### Plate of mandarins — 橘子 (júzi)

`images/Food/Orange_Plate.png` · 64x64 px

- **Meaning:** 橘 sounds like 吉 (luck) and the golden colour means wealth; oranges are given as New Year gifts.
- **Where to place it:** Dining tables and offering tables; a typical gift when visiting relatives.

#### Spring rolls — 春卷 (chūnjuǎn)

`images/Food/Spring_Rolls.png` · 64x64 px

- **Meaning:** Golden rolls that look like gold bars: wealth, and a welcome to spring.
- **Where to place it:** Dinner tables and food stalls.

#### Snack box — 点心盒 (diǎnxīn hé)

`images/Food/Candy_Box.png` · 64x64 px

- **Meaning:** A box of sweets and snacks offered to guests during New Year visits: it wishes them a sweet new year, and the full compartments mean all-round good fortune.
- **Where to place it:** Living-room tables, offered to guests during New Year visits.

#### Hot pot — 火锅 (huǒguō)

`images/Food/Hot_Pot.png` · 64x64 px

- **Meaning:** Everyone eating from one pot: togetherness and a warm, lively reunion.
- **Where to place it:** Centre of a dinner table or restaurant table.

#### Tea set — 茶具 (chájù)

`images/Food/Tea_Set.png` · 64x64 px

- **Meaning:** Serving tea shows respect and hospitality to guests and elders during New Year visits.
- **Where to place it:** Tea houses, guest rooms and study tables.

#### Sky lantern — 孔明灯 (Kǒngmíng dēng)

`images/Items/Sky_Lantern.png` · 64x64 px

- **Meaning:** Released into the night sky carrying wishes up to heaven: hope for the future.

#### Fortune stick cup — 签筒 (qiāntǒng)

#### Calligraphy set — 笔墨 (bǐmò)

`images/Items/Calligraphy_Set.png` · 64x64 px

- **Meaning:** Brush and ink used to write couplets and 福 signs: learning, culture and good wishes.

`images/Plants/Mandarin_Tree.png` · 64x128 px

- **Meaning:** 桔 sounds like 吉 (luck) and the golden fruit means wealth; a classic New Year gift.
- **Where to place it:** Beside front doors and shop entrances, or in indoor corners (symbol of prosperity).

#### Plum blossom tree — 梅花树 (méihuā shù)

`images/Plants/Plum_Tree.png` · 128x128 px

- **Meaning:** Blooms in the winter snow: resilience, hope and the coming of spring.

#### Plum branches in vase — 梅花瓶 (méihuā píng)

`images/Plants/Plum_Vase.png` · 64x128 px

#### Bamboo cluster — 竹子 (zhúzi)

`images/Plants/Bamboo_Cluster.png` · 64x128 px

#### Lucky bamboo pot — 富贵竹 (fùguì zhú)

`images/Plants/Lucky_Bamboo.png` · 64x64 px

#### Narcissus pot — 水仙花 (shuǐxiān huā)

`images/Plants/Narcissus_Pot.png` · 64x64 px

- **Meaning:** The "water immortal" flower blooms at New Year and brings good fortune for the coming year.

#### Green Dragon tile — 發财 (fā cái)

`images/Mahjong/Tile_Green_Dragon.png` · 64x64 px

- **Meaning:** The green dragon tile. 發 is the 发 of 发财 ("get rich"), so it is a favourite New Year symbol of wealth.
- **Where to place it:** UI icon, collectible or reward; fits wealth-themed rewards because 發 means "prosper".

#### One Dot tile — 一筒 (yī tǒng)

`images/Mahjong/Tile_One_Dot.png` · 64x64 px

- **Meaning:** The first tile of the dots suit; its round shape is said to come from an old coin.
- **Where to place it:** UI icon, collectible, reward, or scattered on a table.

#### One Bamboo tile — 幺鸡 (yāo jī)

`images/Mahjong/Tile_One_Bamboo.png` · 64x64 px

- **Meaning:** The first tile of the bamboo suit, traditionally drawn as a bird instead of a bamboo stick.
- **Where to place it:** UI icon, collectible, reward, or a lucky-draw item.
